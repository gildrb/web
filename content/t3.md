# A logomark system for T3

T3 ships more than one product, so the mark had to hold as [`T3.chat`](https://t3.chat/), as [`T3.code`](https://t3.codes/), and on its own in a favicon or an app grid. Nobody commissioned it. I drew it across several months and posted the versions as they came, first in the T3 Discord and then as a [thread on X](https://x.com/gilrdb/status/2080990301208952994). The community, [@r_marked](https://x.com/r_marked), and [@theo](https://x.com/theo) told me which ones were working.

![The T3 logomark](media:t3-mark)

### Hundreds of versions, in public

There are hundreds of drawings behind this mark. Almost all of them were posted the day I made them, and almost all of them are wrong. Drawing the wrong shape is how I find the right one, and doing that in a channel full of people means someone tells me within the hour.

The boards here are where the versions ended up, not where the work happened.

![The exploration canvas](media:t3-canvas-overview)

The T and the 3 want to be one figure instead of two characters standing next to each other. The crossbar of the T carries into the 3, so the pair shares a spine. Finding that meant testing how far the 3 could open, how much the terminals could shear, and how much of the T could go missing before the mark stopped reading as T3.

### Feedback in the open

On 19 March 2026 I posted the application board in the T3 Discord and asked for thoughts.

![The application board posted for feedback](media:t3-feedback-board)

markr came back with the note that set up the next two weeks: "It still feels like there is a bit of excess space between the glyphs, I can't quite put my finger on why though." I asked whether he meant the mark or the type. The mark, he said, the t and the 3.

![Feedback on the spacing between the glyphs](media:t3-feedback-spacing)

By 31 March I was posting frames nine at a time. Carl read the fourth and the last as Ws, which is what happens when the crossbar shears too far. markr relayed that Theo had not liked the angles in the old logo, so the direction that fixed the spacing was reopening a different problem. "Mixed feedback on lots of them makes me go into all kinds of directions," I wrote back. markr's reply was "design by committee".

![Nine frames and the replies they drew](media:t3-feedback-frames)

Some passes went angular and mechanical, with the bowl of the 3 squared off into facets.

![Weight and angle tests](media:t3-weight-tests)
![Ghosted comparison grid](media:t3-ghost-grid)

### Color came last

Pink belongs to T3, but the mark had to survive without it. Every candidate was judged in white on black first and only then given the palette.

![Color passes on the canvas](media:t3-canvas-color)
![Colorway tests](media:t3-color-tests)

### Refining the drawing

The last pass is invisible at a glance. I thinned the joint between the crossbar and the bowl, pulled the bowl tighter, and squared the terminals so the mark keeps its rhythm at favicon size.

![Before and after refinement](media:t3-before-after)

### Scaling across products

A logomark for one product is a drawing. A logomark for a family has to be built out, so I drew the cases where it actually gets used: the two logotypes, the iOS icon on its grid, the favicon ladder down to sixteen pixels, and the lockup on its clear-space frame. The angular version below is the one the server started calling the meat grinder.
